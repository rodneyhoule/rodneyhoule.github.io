AG Caddy privacy policy

Effective October 29, 2026

AG Caddy is a golf app for Awbrey Glen: pace of play, green distances, scoring and games for your group, and Loop Challenges, achievements, leagues and friends for members. This policy explains what the app collects, what it shares, and how long anything is kept. There are no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. A member account is optional and only needed for Challenges, Achievements and Leagues.

On your phone only

These stay on your phone. They are never sent anywhere by the app.

  • Your location during a round, used to tell when you finish each hole. The app uses GPS only from your tee time until the round ends. Before your tee time it keeps an approximate location session so it can switch GPS on at the right moment; those readings are not used or stored.
  • Your location on the Driving Range screen, used only while that screen is open, before a round, to show how far you are from each range flag. It is not stored, and it is not shared on the live map.
  • Round recordings: a log of each round's GPS readings and hole finishes, kept in the app's folder in the Files app so you can review or delete them. The newest 20 are kept.
  • Past rounds: your tee times, hole finish times, and pace for each round you play.
  • Your scores, when you keep score: strokes, putts, penalties, fairways and greens for each hole, and any shots you measure (unless you are signed in as a member; see Loop Challenges and Achievements below).
  • Your player profile: your name, handicap index and preferred tees, used as you in your group.
  • The players in your group, when you add them: each one's name, handicap index and tees, and the players you have played with before, so a regular group is quick to set up, and the game you pick with its amounts.
  • Another group's card kept on your phone, when you play a match and choose This phone: their names, handicap indexes, tees and scores.
  • Settings: your tee time choices, whether you have seen How it works, whether sharing is on, whether pin positions, pace and round stats are shown, and the last pin position the app knew.

Deleting the app deletes all of this.

Shared with other AG Caddy and AG Pace players: the live map

While your round is on, the app shares your group's position so other AG Caddy and AG Pace players on the course can see it on the map. This is on by default and can be turned off at any time with "Share my position" in the app's Settings.

What is shared, about every 10 seconds while moving and every minute while still:

  • your phone's current position and its accuracy
  • your tee time and starting hole
  • the hole you are on, and whether you are on the tee, playing, or at the green
  • your pace (minutes ahead or behind target)
  • whether your group is riding a cart or walking
  • a random ID created by the app for your phone, so your position replaces itself instead of piling up

Not shared: your name, phone number, email, Apple ID, contacts, or anything else about you. Other players see a dot labeled with your tee time only.

When: only from your tee time until your round ends, and only while sharing is on. Nothing is shared before your tee time, after your round, or when the app is not tracking a round.

How long it is kept: your position is deleted when your round ends. If that does not happen (for example, your phone runs out of battery), other players stop seeing it after 10 minutes without an update, and it is deleted within about an hour. Only your latest position is ever stored; there is no history of where you have been.

Where it is stored: with Supabase, a hosted database service, in its West US (Oregon) region. Supabase stores it on the app's behalf and does not use it for anything else. The random ID is an anonymous sign in that exists only so your phone can update its own position and no one else's.

Shared with other AG Caddy and AG Pace players: the day's pin position

When you set the day's pin position (1 to 4) next to your tee time, it is sent to the same Supabase database so every AG Caddy and AG Pace player sees it for the rest of the day, and the app can guess tomorrow's. Only the course, the date, the position, and when it was set are stored. Nothing about you is stored with it: no ID, no name, and no location. The app reads it when the first screen opens. With "Show pin positions" off in the app's Settings, the app neither reads nor sends it.

Shared with the other group: a match with a code

When you play a match against another group and their card is kept on their phone, one phone makes the match and gets a four character code, and a phone in the other group joins with it. Each phone then sends its group's players (the names as typed, handicap index and tees), its scores, the game and its amounts, and its tee time, so the other phone can show the match. Only the two phones in the match can read it; each changes only its own group.

Where it is stored: with Supabase, in the same database as the live map, under the same anonymous sign in (no account, no email). A match is deleted 7 days after the day it was played, and the phone that made it can delete it sooner by removing the group. Nothing is sent when you keep their card on your own phone.

Your member account (optional)

You do not need an account to use AG Caddy. Only Leagues, Challenges and Achievements ask for one. To sign up (or sign in again on a new phone), you type your first name, last name and email, and then a six digit code that is emailed to that address. Once you are signed in, your name in the app is the name you signed up with.

What is kept: nothing about you is kept until you type the right code. To send the code, our sign in service (Supabase Auth) holds the email you typed; if no account comes of it, it is deleted within an hour. Your name stays on your phone until the code is right. Then your account is kept in the same Supabase database: your first and last name, your email, and when you signed up. Your account can later also hold your handicap index and GHIN number; GHIN numbers are never sent to phones. Your email is used only to send sign in codes, which are delivered by Resend, an email delivery service that sends them on the app's behalf. Your email is never shown to other members and never sent to anyone else's phone. Your name is seen only by signed in members: when they search for members to add as friends or invite to a league, and, if you join Loop Challenges, on its boards. Two people who share one email can each have an account on it; anyone who can read that inbox can sign in as either.

Loop Challenges: if you join Loop Challenges, each counted round you play (one a day, started from Loop Challenges) sends its five hole scores and the day it was played to the same database, with your member account. Signed in members see your name on the boards, with your score to par, your birdies and the holes you have birdied, and can open your best round's card (each hole's strokes, your birdies, how many counted rounds you played). Putts, fairways and greens are never sent, nor where you were or how long the round took. The time a counted round started is kept only to allow one a day and to order ties, and is never shown. Leaving Loop Challenges deletes your counted rounds; deleting your account does too.

Achievements: while you are signed in, each round you play sends your own scores to the same database when it ends, with your member account and the day: your strokes on each hole, your putts and whether you hit each green (only with Keep round stats on), how long the round took from your tee time to the last green (18 holes only), and, in a skins game, how many holes you won. They are used only to work out your achievements, which only you see. Nothing is sent for the other players in your group, nor where you were. Rounds played signed out are never sent. Deleting your account deletes these rounds.

Your index and tees: while you are signed in, the handicap index and preferred tees in your profile are sent to the same database with your member account when they change, so the members of your leagues and members who add you as a friend see them. Nothing else from your profile is sent.

Leagues: a league you start or join is kept in the same database: its name, days, season and games, its schedule (days skipped and dates added), its members with their roles (owner, admin, member), the invites, who signed up for each league day, and each league day's teams, groups (starting holes or tee times) and no shows. A private league is seen only by its members and the members invited to it; a public one is listed for every signed in member. A league's members see each other's names and handicap indexes, who is playing each league day, and its teams and groups. A league's admins can add a player who has no account (a guest): the first and last name, handicap index and tees the admin types, and the date the index was set. The admin is responsible for having that player's OK. A guest's name and index are seen by the league's members, their rounds are posted by the member who kept their card, and an admin can remove a guest (with their rounds) at any time; deleting the league deletes its guests. A round played from a league day (Play today's round) sends the scores of you and the league's members and guests on your card (18 holes, or the day's nine), with each player's tees and handicap index, so the league's members see each day's results, who owes whom, and the season standings. Nothing else from the round is sent (no putts, fairways, times or places). You, whoever posted your round, or an admin can take it off. Leaving a league removes you from it and your sign ups from today on (your posted rounds stay in its results); an admin can remove you too. Deleting your account deletes the leagues you own (with everything in them), removes you from the others, and deletes your league rounds.

Friends: the members you add as friends are kept in the same database with your member account. Adding a member needs nothing from them, and they are not told. You see your friends' names, handicap indexes and preferred tees, and any signed in member who adds you sees yours. Removing a friend deletes them from your list; deleting your account deletes your list and takes you off everyone else's.

Your profile picture: if you set one (Settings, Profile picture), the photo you take or pick is cropped on your phone to a circle and sent as a small image (512 by 512, with nothing else from the photo, such as where or when it was taken) to private storage in the same Supabase project, with your member account. Signed in members see it wherever you show in the app (your group, leagues, friends, Loop Challenges); it is never shown on the live map, on the lock screen or on the round cards you share, and never to AG Pace players or anyone not signed in. Remove photo in Settings deletes it, and so does deleting your account. Phones keep the photos they have shown in their cache, which iOS may clear at any time; a removed photo is gone from other phones within a few minutes of their next look.

League images: a league's owner or admins can set an image for it, cropped on their phone and kept the same way. It is seen by the league's members and the members invited to it, or by every signed in member for a public league. Removing it, or deleting the league, deletes it.

Reporting and blocking: a long press on another member's photo or a league's image offers Report and Block member. A report sends the member or league it is about, your member name, the screen you were on and the app and iPhone details listed under Bug reports, and is emailed to Rodney, who removes photos that are not of their member or are offensive (usually within a day) and can stop a member setting another. Blocking a member is kept with your account: you do not see each other's photos, and they cannot add you as a friend. Your blocked members are listed in Settings, where you can unblock them; deleting your account deletes the list.

On your phone: your sign in is kept in the iPhone Keychain, not shared with the live map. Your position on the map stays anonymous whether or not you are signed in.

Signing out and deleting: Sign out in the app's Settings removes the sign in from your phone. Delete my account deletes your account: your name, your email, and everything kept with it. A record of each code sent, each wrong code and each new account (a coded fingerprint of the email and of the network address, never the address itself, with the phone's anonymous ID and the time) is kept for at most a day, to stop anyone flooding an inbox.

Bug reports and feature requests

Only when you send one: the bug button on home, or Report a bug or idea in the round's menu. A report carries what you write, whether it is a bug or a feature request, the screen you were on (and the hole, in a round), a screenshot of that screen (on unless you switch it off; it can show names, scores or the map), the app's version and build, your iPhone model and iOS version, your phone's anonymous ID, and your member name if you are signed in. It is kept in the same Supabase database, with the screenshot in private storage, and emailed to Rodney (through Resend, which delivers the email). Only Rodney reads them; they are not shared. Reports are deleted after a year, and Delete my account deletes yours. With no signal, a report waits on your phone until it can be sent, then is deleted from the phone. A phone can send at most 10 a day.

Photos

When you share a round card, you choose where it goes. Saving it to Photos uses the permission iOS asks for at that moment. When you set a profile picture or a league image, Take photo uses the camera (iOS asks first) and Choose photo opens iOS's photo picker, which gives the app only the photo you pick. The app never reads the rest of your photo library.

Children

AG Caddy is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date, and the app will show How it works again when what is shared changes.
